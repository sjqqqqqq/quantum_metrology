\begin{abstract}
We describe the progression of the model used to estimate a three-component field
from a randomly controlled spin ensemble: separate-beam readout with spin flips
but no loss (v5), one-beam readout with spin flips (v6), and the current one-beam
model with cesium-derived coefficients and Raman loss (v7). We specify the
implemented equations, cluster configuration, and analysis of the four v7 datasets.
The central comparison switches the tensor Hamiltonian on and off at fixed
scattering and readout noise. The saved results show shorter times to a target
filtered variance with the tensor term, with gains that depend on the target,
probe strength, and optical depth.
\end{abstract}

\section{Common estimation problem and model progression}

We model one spin $J$ in an initially uncorrelated ensemble of $N_a$ atoms. The
unknown static field is $\bm w=(w_x,w_y,w_z)$. With $\hbar=1$,
\begin{equation}
 H(t)=\bm w\cdot\bm J+
 \Omega[\cos\phi(t)J_x+\sin\phi(t)J_y]
 +\frac{\beta_H}{2J}J_z^2 .
 \label{eq:H}
\end{equation}
The factor $1/(2J)$ is part of the code convention: \code{beta} is not the
coefficient multiplying $J_z^2$ directly. Frequencies are in units of $\Omega$,
time is plotted as $\Omega t$, and the scripts set $\Omega=1$. The phase is
uniform on $[0,2\pi)$, constant for blocks of 1,000 steps, and independently
drawn for each realization. The runs discussed here use $J=3$,
$\rho_0=|J,+x\rangle\langle J,+x|$, and observable $O_0=J_y$.

A record contains a known control sequence and a signal normalized to the
\emph{initial} atom number:
\begin{equation}
 M_n=s_n(\bm w)+\xi_n,\qquad
 \xi_n\sim\mathcal N(0,\sigma^2),\qquad
 s_n=\Tr[O_n(\bm w)\rho_0].
 \label{eq:measurement}
\end{equation}
Noise samples are independent within a record. The forward model evolves an
ensemble expectation, rather than individual scattering trajectories or a
conditional collective quantum state.

\section{Separate beams without manifold loss: v5}
\label{sec:separate}

\subsection{Scattering from the tensor beam}

The first open-system model retains two within-manifold Raman spin-flip operators,
\begin{equation}
 L_+=\sqrt{\gamma}J_+,\qquad L_-=\sqrt{\gamma}J_-,\qquad
 J_\pm=J_x\pm iJ_y,\qquad \gamma=\frac{\beta}{\eta},\quad \beta_H=\beta.
 \label{eq:jumps}
\end{equation}
The master equation is
\begin{align}
 \dot\rho&=-i[H(t),\rho]+\Dsf[\rho],\\
 \Dsf[\rho]&=\gamma\sum_{\nu=\pm}
 \left(J_\nu\rho J_\nu^\dagger-\tfrac12\{J_\nu^\dagger J_\nu,\rho\}\right)
 =-\gamma\sum_{k=x,y}[J_k,[J_k,\rho]].
 \label{eq:sf}
\end{align}
This channel preserves trace and is unital. Its adjoint has the same operator
form, with exact dissipative identities
\begin{equation}
 \Dsf^\dagger[J_x]=-\gamma J_x,\qquad
 \Dsf^\dagger[J_y]=-\gamma J_y,\qquad
 \Dsf^\dagger[J_z]=-2\gamma J_z.
 \label{eq:relaxation}
\end{equation}
Thus $\gamma$ is a transverse spin-relaxation coefficient, not the total photon
scattering rate. The tensor Hamiltonian couples first moments to higher moments;
these identities do not close the full driven dynamics.

Far from the excited hyperfine structure, the tensor shift and scattering both
scale as intensity divided by detuning squared. This motivates a fixed ratio
$\eta=\beta/\gamma$; the original run chose $\eta=30$. This phenomenological
spin-flip ratio differs from the total-scattering ratio $\eta_s$ introduced below.
The optical-pumping and light-shift framework is described in Ref.~\cite{deutsch};
our numerical conventions are specified by the repository scripts.

\subsection{Independent readout and its limitation}

A separate readout beam supplies $N_p$ photons per interval and Faraday gain $G$
per atom per unit spin. The model uses
\begin{equation}
 \sigma^2=\frac{1}{N_pG^2N_a^2},
 \label{eq:separate-noise}
\end{equation}
independently of $\beta$, and omits readout-induced scattering. The dataset
\code{data\_mle\_over\_time\_decoherence\_1} uses $\sigma=0.021$ and
$\beta\in\{0,0.5,1,2,5\}$. At $\beta=0$, both the tensor shift and modeled
spin flips vanish, while finite-noise readout remains available. The reference
is therefore an idealized decoherence-free measurement.

A physical readout beam also scatters. In a two-beam extension its contribution
would have its own polarization, detuning, and branching channels; replacing
$\gamma$ by $\gamma+\gamma_r$ is sufficient only if it has the same reduced
spin-flip structure. The next model instead uses one beam for both control and
measurement. Omitting \code{--eta} in v5 sets the dissipative map to the identity
and recovers the v4 closed-system path.

\section{One beam without manifold loss: v6}
\label{sec:onebeam}

The same probe now generates the tensor shift, scatters, and supplies the Faraday
readout. Sweeping its strength must change the noise as well as the relaxation:
\begin{equation}
 \gamma=\frac{\beta}{\eta},\qquad
 \gamma\sigma^2\dt=\frac{1}{\OD_{\mathrm{eff}}N_a},\qquad
 \sigma^2(\beta)=\frac{\eta}{\OD_{\mathrm{eff}}N_a\dt\,\beta}.
 \label{eq:v6}
\end{equation}
Here $\OD_{\mathrm{eff}}$ absorbs the conversion between spin-flip relaxation
and Faraday measurement strength; it is not automatically the resonant optical
depth. The dataset \code{data\_mle\_over\_time\_onebeam\_1} uses
$\eta=30$, $\OD_{\mathrm{eff}}=10$, and $N_a=2\times10^5$.

Increasing $\beta$ strengthens nonlinear dynamics and reduces shot noise, but
also shortens the useful signal duration. At $\beta=0$ there is no probe and
$\sigma^2$ diverges, so v6/v7 exclude zero from the sweep. To isolate the tensor
term, \code{--tensor-scale 0} sets $\beta_H=0$ while retaining the rates and
noise of a nonzero $\beta$. This is a numerical control at fixed measurement
strength, not a zero-intensity experiment.

The illustrative envelope $\partial s/\partial w\sim t e^{-\gamma t}$ gives
$F\sim\sigma^{-2}\int_0^T t^2e^{-2\gamma t}\,dt$. Information initially grows
with probe strength but saturates as sensitivity decays, suggesting a scale
$\beta\sim\eta/T$. This is a heuristic rather than an exact optimum for the
three-parameter random-control problem. Direction-dependent sensitivity and
the tensor dynamics must be computed.

\section{One beam with Raman loss and atomic coefficients: v7}
\label{sec:loss}

\subsection{Rates and signed tensor shift}

The current model adds Raman transfer from the chosen ground hyperfine manifold
to the other manifold. The supporting calculation
\code{cs\_light\_shift\_scattering\_ratios.py} constructs cesium dipole matrices
from angular-momentum coefficients and the atomic data conventions of
Ref.~\cite{steck}. For a linearly polarized ($\pi$) probe it adiabatically
eliminates one excited fine-structure manifold, checks dipole closure and
scattering normalization, and extracts the tensor shift, within-manifold
relaxation, loss operator, and vector-to-scalar polarizability ratio.

The v7 script uses rounded presets from that calculation at detuning $-20$ GHz
from the selected ground-manifold-to-excited-centroid transition. They are
effective far-detuned coefficients, not a full detuning-dependent pumping model.
Let $\gamma_s$ be the scattering-rate scale normalized to the initial $+x$
coherent state, and $q_v=b/a$ the vector-to-scalar polarizability ratio per unit
magnetic quantum number. Then
\begin{align}
 \gamma_s&=\frac{\beta}{|\eta_s|},&
 \gamma_{\mathrm{sf}}&=r_{\mathrm{within}}\gamma_s,\\
 \beta_H&=s_T\operatorname{sgn}(\eta_s)\beta,&
 K_L&=s_L\gamma_s(a_L\id+c_LJ_z^2),
 \label{eq:v7-rates}\\
 \sigma^2&=\frac{1}{q_v^2\OD_{\mathrm{res}}N_a\dt\,\gamma_s},&
 \OD_{\mathrm{res}}&=\frac{N_a\sigma_{\mathrm{res}}}{A_{\mathrm{beam}}}.
 \label{eq:v7-noise}
\end{align}
The CLI sweep variable $\beta$ is positive; $\eta_s$ supplies the tensor-shift
sign. The flags \code{--tensor-scale} and \code{--loss-scale} set $s_T$ and
$s_L$, both one by default. The resonant cross section must use the same
normalization as the Faraday-noise convention in Eq.~\eqref{eq:v7-noise}.

The coefficient $r_{\mathrm{within}}$ multiplies the $J_\pm$ dissipator. It is a
normalized transverse-relaxation coefficient, not the probability of a
within-manifold event per scattered photon. Likewise $\gamma_s$ is a fixed
reference scale; instantaneous scattering of an evolving state need not equal
its initial-state value.

\subsection{Trace-decreasing master equation and measured signal}

Using Eq.~\eqref{eq:sf} at rate $\gamma_{\mathrm{sf}}$, the reduced state obeys
\begin{equation}
 \dot\rho=-i[H,\rho]+\Dsf[\rho]-\tfrac12\{K_L,\rho\}.
 \label{eq:loss-me}
\end{equation}
Here $K_L=\sum_q(L_q^L)^\dagger L_q^L$ for transfer operators $L_q^L$ into
the other manifold. Their recycling terms lie outside the modeled space and
are omitted. Thus
\begin{equation}
 p=\Tr\rho,\qquad \dot p=-\Tr[K_L\rho],\qquad
 \Tr[O\rho]=p\Tr[O\rho_{\mathrm{cond}}],\quad \rho_{\mathrm{cond}}=\rho/p.
 \label{eq:survival}
\end{equation}
The unnormalized expectation is the signal per initial atom if transferred
atoms are dark. It must not be renormalized to the surviving population before
adding the noise in Eq.~\eqref{eq:measurement}.

Loss is state dependent because $c_L\ne0$. Even without spin flips, conditioning
on survival changes the state through non-Hermitian attenuation; it need not
follow the lossless unitary trajectory. Only uniform loss $K_L=\kappa\id$
factors into $e^{-\kappa t}$ times otherwise unchanged dynamics. In particular,
$e^{-\langle K_L\rangle_0t}$, used as a survival estimate in the notebook's
initial rate table, is not an exact survival curve for the driven model.

For the initial $+x$ coherent state, $\langle J_z^2\rangle_0=J/2$. The initial
\emph{dissipative} relaxation rate of $\langle J_x\rangle$ is
\begin{equation}
 R_0=\gamma_{\mathrm{sf}}+\langle K_L\rangle_0
 =\gamma_s[r_{\mathrm{within}}+s_L(a_L+c_LJ/2)].
 \label{eq:R0}
\end{equation}
This is saved as \code{gamma\_array.npy}; it differs from
\code{gamma\_sf\_array.npy}. For Cs D2, $F=3$, with $s_L=1$,
\begin{equation}
 \langle K_L\rangle_0=0.22005\gamma_s,\quad
 R_0=0.22555\gamma_s=0.0306038\beta,\quad \beta/R_0=32.68.
\end{equation}
Loss contributes $97.56\%$ of this initial relaxation. This percentage is not
the branching fraction of all scattering events, and $R_0^{-1}$ is a reference
time scale, not a universal exponential decay time.

\subsection{Relation to the previous one-beam run}

Identifying the v6 rate with $R_0$ gives the noise-matching convention
\begin{equation}
 \OD_{\mathrm{eff}}=
 \frac{q_v^2}{R_0/\gamma_s}\OD_{\mathrm{res}}
 =0.07151\OD_{\mathrm{res}}\quad\text{(Cs D2, $F=3$)}.
\end{equation}
Thus OD 140 approximately reproduces the earlier effective OD 10. The actual
comparison is close but not exact: v6 uses $\beta/\gamma=30$, v7 has
$\beta/R_0=32.68$, and the v7 noise standard deviation is $4.3\%$ larger.
These are nearby operating points with different dissipators, not an exact
matching of every effective quantity.

As an algebraic check, $s_L=0$ removes loss. For $s_T=1$, positive $\eta_s$,
and overrides satisfying $r_{\mathrm{within}}/\eta_s=1/\eta$ and
$q_v^2\OD_{\mathrm{res}}/\eta_s=\OD_{\mathrm{eff}}/\eta$, the v7 equations
reduce to v6. Setting only $s_T=0$ preserves both scattering and noise.

\section{Numerical propagation and parameter fitting}
\label{sec:numerics}

The adjoint irreversible generator is
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal D^\dagger[O]=
 -\gamma_{\mathrm{sf}}\sum_{k=x,y}[J_k,[J_k,O]]-\tfrac12\{K_L,O\}.
\end{equation}
For row-major vectorization,
$\operatorname{vec}_C(XOY)=(X\otimes Y^{\mathsf T})\operatorname{vec}_C(O)$.
The script exponentiates this superoperator to obtain
$\mathcal A=\exp(\dt\mathcal D^\dagger)$, a $49\times49$ matrix at $J=3$.
It is constant within a realization and independent of $\bm w$.

With $U_w=e^{-i\dt\bm w\cdot\bm J}$ and $U_c[n]=e^{-i\dt H_c[n]}$, the
implemented update is
\begin{equation}
 O_{n+1}=U_w^\dagger U_c[n]^\dagger\mathcal A[O_n]U_c[n]U_w.
 \label{eq:implemented}
\end{equation}
The dissipative substep is exact; separating dissipation from the unitaries,
and field from control, are first-order splittings. Without spin flips,
$\mathcal A[O]=e^{-\dt K_L/2}Oe^{-\dt K_L/2}$. With both channels present,
$\mathcal A[\id]$ is generally not $e^{-\dt K_L}$, since the spin-flip and
loss maps need not commute.

\paragraph{Time-ordering convention.}
Successive adjoint maps act on the observable in increasing array-index order.
If $\mathcal E_n$ is the dual state map, after two steps
\begin{equation}
 \Tr[\mathcal E_1^\dagger\mathcal E_0^\dagger(O_0)\rho_0]
 =\Tr[O_0\mathcal E_0\mathcal E_1(\rho_0)].
\end{equation}
This reverses the control order relative to forward Schr\"odinger propagation
with that indexed sequence. Data generation, residuals, and Fisher derivatives
all share this convention. The results describe that implemented protocol;
identifying its entire record with a chronologically driven experiment requires
a separate ordering check. Single-step duality alone does not establish equality
of the complete time-ordered records.

Fr\'echet derivatives of $U_w$ generate $\partial O_n/\partial w_a$ by the
product rule; $\mathcal A$ acts on both the observable and its derivatives.
For known control realization $i$ the classical Fisher matrix is
\begin{equation}
 F_{i,ab}(T)=\sigma^{-2}\sum_{n\le T}
 \frac{\partial s_{i,n}}{\partial w_a}
 \frac{\partial s_{i,n}}{\partial w_b}.
 \label{eq:fisher}
\end{equation}
Atomic coefficients, optical depth, atom number, and noise are treated as known.

At each cutoff the estimator minimizes $C=\frac12\sum_n(M_n-s_n)^2$ using
SciPy's bounded trust-region least-squares method with $-1\le w_a\le1$.
Starting from $\bm w=0$, it fits successively longer prefixes (250 steps, then
doubling, ending at the cutoff). Cutoffs do not share fitted estimates.
An excessive final cost triggers up to two random restarts, keeping the
lowest-cost fit. Controls, noise, and restart points are drawn before joblib
dispatch. Each realization reuses its record across cutoffs.

\section{Interpretation of variances, filtering, and Fisher bounds}
\label{sec:statistics}

For a fixed true field and known control, the local unbiased-estimator bound is
$\Cov(\hat{\bm w}\mid i)\succeq F_i^{-1}$. Two averages must be distinguished:
\begin{equation}
 B_{\mathrm{cond}}=\E_i[F_i^{-1}],\qquad
 B_{\mathrm{avg}}=(\E_i[F_i])^{-1},\qquad
 B_{\mathrm{cond}}\succeq B_{\mathrm{avg}}.
 \label{eq:bounds}
\end{equation}
The inequality assumes positive-definite information matrices. The conditional
bound is the benchmark for locally unbiased estimates made separately for
each control record. Inverse average information instead pertains to an
averaged-information experiment, such as joint estimation from many records
with the appropriate record-count factor. It is not the bound for an arbitrary
fixed control sequence.

For conditionally unbiased estimates, total covariance equals the average
conditional covariance. Control-dependent bias, finite samples, parameter
bounds, and selection complicate the comparison. Agreement with
$B_{\mathrm{cond}}$ is consequently an empirical diagnostic, not proof of
unbiasedness or global identifiability.

The script retains all 300 fits. The notebook subsequently rejects those above
\begin{equation}
 C_{\mathrm{band}}=\frac{\sigma^2}{2}(n+5\sqrt{2n}),\qquad
 \E[C(\bm w_{\mathrm{true}})]=\frac{n\sigma^2}{2},
 \label{eq:costband}
\end{equation}
where $n$ counts measurements at the cutoff. This noise-based threshold is
referenced to the cost at the truth; passing it does not guarantee the correct
minimum. Surviving fits give sample variances with denominator
$N_{\mathrm{kept}}-1$. The notebook computes statistics separately for each
true-field draw, then averages the summaries across 20 tasks. It does not pool
estimates of different true fields into a single covariance.

The plotted quantity is filtered total variance
$V(T)=\sum_a\mathrm{Var}(\hat w_a)$, excluding squared bias. Both Fisher
summaries retain all realizations, so the empirical variance and bounds refer
to different selected populations when fits are rejected. Mean rejection
fractions over the beta/time grid are $3.0\%$, $3.8\%$, $4.5\%$, and $5.6\%$
for OD 140 on/off and OD 10 on/off, respectively; the largest task-averaged
fraction at a single setting is $13.2\%$. Dashed curves show
$\Tr B_{\mathrm{cond}}$ and dotted curves $\Tr B_{\mathrm{avg}}$.
The time-to-target metric is filtered variance, not total mean-squared error.

\section{Cluster configuration and v7 results}

\subsection{Variance relative to the conditional benchmark}

Figure~\ref{fig:overview} compares the four datasets. At $T=14$ in the OD 140
tensor-on run, $V/\Tr B_{\mathrm{cond}}=0.99$--$1.03$ across all six probe
strengths. This is close agreement for the filtered estimator at that operating
point. It does not establish efficiency for every time or configuration.
For example, in the OD 10 tensor-on run this ratio is about 17 at $\beta=0.5$
and 6.6 at $\beta=20$, while intermediate strengths remain near one.

Tensor-off estimates can also remain far above their conditional bounds:
the final ratio at $\beta=20$ is about 17 for OD 140 and 92 for OD 10.
A large gap can reflect nonlinear estimation errors, weak identifiability,
bias, or optimizer behavior missed by the cost test. These data do not uniquely
diagnose the cause. Conditional Fisher comparisons help distinguish local
information gain from the additional estimator-performance gap.

\begin{equation}

gains additionally include settings where tensor-off fits do not reach their
own conditional benchmark.

\subsection{Comparison with spin-flip-only relaxation}

Figure~\ref{fig:lossvssf} compares v7 OD 140 tensor-on with v6 \code{onebeam\_1}.
At $T=14$, filtered-variance ratios v7/v6 are approximately $0.84$, $0.44$, and
$0.075$ at $\beta=0.5,1,2$; at $\beta\ge5$ they are of order $10^{-3}$.
The loss-dominated reduced model therefore performs much better than the
spin-flip-only model at these approximately matched operating points.

A single initial relaxation rate is insufficient to predict estimation
performance. This comparison does not mean that adding loss to a fixed
spin-flip model improves precision: v7 reduces the spin-flip coefficient while
introducing loss. It also does not imply unitary dynamics of survivors,

\section{Scope of the current model and next checks}

The current configuration is a single-beam, single-manifold mean-signal model
with atom-derived coefficients, spin flips, and state-dependent Raman loss.
The conclusions above concern its paired v7 datasets and implemented control
convention. The following extensions would strengthen the physical and
statistical interpretation.

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Both ground manifolds.} Transferred atoms are treated as dark and
never return. A fuller cesium model would evolve $F=3\oplus4$ (dimension 16),
include inter-manifold pumping and both optical responses, and use a
$256\times256$ operator space.
\item \textbf{Measurement fluctuations.} The likelihood adds independent photon
shot noise to a deterministic ensemble mean. Atomic projection noise,
stochastic loss-number fluctuations, conditional measurement back-action,
and induced interatomic correlations are not separately simulated. Faraday
coupling and optical depth also require calibration for the detection geometry.
\item \textbf{Ordering and numerical convergence.} Compare the convention in
Section~\ref{sec:numerics} with forward state propagation before interpreting
records as a chronological experiment. Quantify splitting error through a
step-size convergence study.
\item \textbf{Estimator diagnostics.} Compare unfiltered variance, bias,
mean-squared error, rejection fractions, and conditional information. Add
uncertainty estimates and targeted checks of restarts and identifiability before
assigning a unique cause to the tensor-off excess variance.
\item \textbf{Broader optimization.} The results cover six constant probe
strengths, one atomic preset, two optical depths, and $T\le14$. Other presets
and time-dependent probe schedules have not been tested by these four datasets.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Repository map and reproducibility}

The root-level v5/v6 decoherence scripts implement the earlier stages;
the v7 script is identified in Section~\ref{sec:results}. Matching
shell launchers specify \code{--od-res 140} or \code{10} and the

\code{loss\_rate\_scs\_array.npy}, and \code{sn\_sd\_array.npy} record the

records; \code{phi\_kept.npy} stores compressed phases.
\code{fisher.npy} stores average information, whereas
\code{fisher\_inv\_realavg.npy} stores averaged individual inverses.
Raw fit arrays have leading axes trial, beta, cutoff, realization; Fisher
summaries have already averaged over realizations.

The v7 notebook filters and averages directly from the task folders. The three
PDFs under \code{latex/figures/} are existing analysis figures. Numerical
summaries in this note were checked against the saved arrays using the
